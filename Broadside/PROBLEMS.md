# Broadside: problems and solutions

Problems met while building the ship designer and the ships built from the hammer, kept so they are not met again
(the launched ships' were met in the prototype, `AssetLab/AssetLab/Keelwright`, and came back with its code).

## Keel looks like stone
- **Problem:** the Meadows keel reads as stone.
- **Cause:** keel textures painted with stone ballast on top.
- **Solution:** keels repainted as wood; the second material only as fittings.

## Rudder linkage out of reach of the deck
- **Problem:** a rudder's tiller could not be reached from the deck; on a capped post the tiller ran through the cap.
- **Cause:** the tiller always sat over the post's top, which on most sterns is 2–3 m over the deck, behind the rail, or inside a post cap.
- **Solution:** each rudder picks its link to the deck from the stern's own pieces (sphere sweeps at helm 0 and ±35°): a tiller over the post when it clears, else a yoke over the post's top with lines (rope) or rods (iron, black metal, flametal) round the post's sides, through or over the rail, to a deck tiller on its own post at hand height. The owner saves it in the rudder's ZDO (`kw_rudder_lay`), so peers and the blueprint get it. In `KwRudderLay*.cs`, `KwRudderLink*.cs`, `KwRudderSweep.cs`.

## Sails shimmer like a mirage
- **Problem:** small fast ripples on hoisted sails look like heat haze, not wind.
- **Cause:** flutter at 1.4–2.4 Hz on short waves, and every face lit flat on its own.
- **Solution:** slow travelling billows, a wind-driven belly, luffing only head to wind or reefed, gusts; welded smooth normals. In `KwSailMotion.cs`, `KwSailWind.cs`, `KwSailWeld.cs`. (The game's own sails are MagicaCloth 2, which fits our rebuilt-on-hoist rig badly.)

## Deck seams flicker
- **Problem:** the planking's and decks' thin, dark board seams flickered on and off, strobing on a curved hull.
- **Cause:** the textures were point filtered, as the game's are, and the seams are thinner than a texel at the low angle a deck is seen at (12 % of a deck's pixels flickering frame to frame).
- **Solution:** the kit's own textures are filtered smoothly with mipmaps and anisotropic filtering (under 3 %). In `KwTextures.cs`.

## Ship hull strobes / looks like a mirage
- **Problem:** dark streaks flicker along the hull and deck of a launched ship; the hull looks wavy.
- **Cause:** the game's building shader (`Custom/Piece`) adds a world-space value noise per vertex. A moving ship slides through it.
- **Solution:** ship copies of each material, set like the vanilla longship's: `_ValueNoise` 0, `_ValueNoiseVertex` 0 (keyword off), `_RippleDistance` 0, `_MoveableObject` 1 + `EnableKeyword("_MOVEABLEOBJECT_ON")`. In `KwShipMaterials.cs`.
- **Proof:** flickering pixels 21% before, 1.3% after, vanilla longship 1.5%.

## Launched ship sinks at login
- **Problem:** the ship lies on the sea floor after logging in.
- **Cause:** the water loads after the ship; the float found no water and the ship fell below the sea's water volume.
- **Solution:** sample water at sea level over each point; hold the ship still until water is found (15 s max). In `KwShipHydro.cs`.

## Hatch on a launched ship can't be used
- **Problem:** E does nothing on a ship's hatch.
- **Cause:** the game hovers the collider's own object only if it is hoverable, else the ship's root.
- **Solution:** a relay component on each fitting collider. In `KwHoverForward.cs`.

## Error every frame on launched ships
- **Problem:** `NullReferenceException` in `ShipEffects` every frame.
- **Cause:** the karve's water-surface and wake objects were deleted; `ShipEffects` uses them.
- **Solution:** keep them, hide their renderers and stop their particles. In `KwShipPrefab.cs`.

## Huge ship floats wrong (wobbles, swamps, tips)
- **Problem:** a 32 m ship rolled ±12° every 2 s on calm water; big hulls swamped or tipped.
- **Cause:** float points act as stiff springs on top of the game's float, which is tuned for a karve; nothing damps the roll.
- **Solution:** real hull buoyancy: the hull in slices from its planking; each step every slice's wet area under the sea (both sides) lifts it at its centre (Archimedes), its sides damped. Mass = water displaced at the design waterline. The game's ship step is replaced for our ships (same controls, sail, rudder, oars). In `KwShipHydro.cs`, `KwHullSlices.cs`, `KwSectionMath.cs`, `KwShipDrive.cs`.

## Ship tips half into the water / wave sounds
- **Problem:** ship heels hard; constant splash sounds.
- **Cause:** no righting force; the karve's splash triggers and water-impact hits sat at the karve's hull positions.
- **Solution:** righting comes from the hull buoyancy; splash triggers removed; water impacts off. In `KwShipHydro.cs`, `KwShipPrefab.cs`.

## Rope and sail twitching
- **Problem:** sails shake while sailing.
- **Cause:** sail lag computed from frame-to-frame drawn positions (noisy).
- **Solution:** use the ship body's velocity, smoothed; cap lag and flutter. In `KwSailMotion.cs`, `KwSailSwing.cs`.

## Sea drawn inside the hull
- **Problem:** the sea surface shows inside the hold.
- **Cause:** no water mask (the karve's went with its hull); hold below the waterline.
- **Solution:** a water mask filling the hull (game's `watermask` material); waterline kept under the lowest deck. In `KwShipWaterMask.cs`, `KwShipTuning.cs`.

## Camera goes through the deck below decks
- **Problem:** under a low deck the camera clips into the deck above.
- **Cause:** the game's camera ignores the vehicle layer.
- **Solution:** with the player's eyes under one of our decks or roofs, the vehicle layer blocks the camera; in the open the camera is raised out of a shut-in space instead (see "Zoomed-out camera snaps in on a ship"). In `KwShipCamera.cs`.

## Helmsman's elbow flips
- **Problem:** the helmsman's arm seemed to stretch and snap, the elbow flipping from side to side.
- **Cause:** the elbow aimed at a fixed point beside the shoulder, which lay near the shoulder-to-fist line whenever the fist came back to the hip.
- **Solution:** the elbow points out to his side, back and a little down, square to the shoulder-to-fist line; the fist goes on the peg by two-bone IK (the LimbIk library). In `KwHelmArms.cs`.

## Ship sails stern-first
- **Problem:** a ship built on loose keel blocks sailed rudder end first; the helmsman faced the rudder.
- **Cause:** the ship's bow was the dock's forward, and keel blocks have no fixed bow end.
- **Solution:** the rudder's end is the stern; with no rudder, the sails' net power points the bow. Blueprints are turned round on read and save. In `KwBpBow.cs`.

## Build menu reads past its slots (found in the game's code)
- **Problem:** the game's build menu shows a piece's crafting station in the slot after its last requirement (`Hud.SetupPieceInfo`), so a piece with a requirement in every slot would read past the end.
- **Cause:** a saved ship's cost can have more kinds of material than the menu has slots.
- **Solution:** the saved ship's piece carries only as many requirements as the menu shows beside the workbench; the rest are checked and taken at the click. In `KwBpCost.cs`, `KwBpPlace.cs`.

## Sea vanishes round the bow
- **Problem:** at the very front of a launched ship the sea had see-through spots down to the seabed.
- **Cause:** the water mask was a straight block per hull slice, each the slice's aft section; where the hull narrows into the stem the block stood outside the planking and hid the sea there.
- **Solution:** the mask is lofted from each section to the next (every section resampled to the same 32 points), so it narrows with the hull. In `KwShipWaterMask.cs`, `KwHullSlices.Rings`.

## Ship rides high out of the water
- **Problem:** a launched ship floated with three quarters of its hull above the sea.
- **Cause:** the design waterline was a quarter of the hull's depth.
- **Solution:** 55% of the depth (40% still looked high), still under the lowest deck so a hold stays dry. In `KwShipTuning.Draught`.

## Stuck in the hold
- **Problem:** below an open hatch the player could barely move, worst in the middle.
- **Cause:** a hold had no floor: the player stood in the hull's V on the keel's ridge with a rib across every metre. Also the hold was about 1 m high; the player is 1.85 m.
- **Solution:** a flat floor of boards over the ribs and keel in every planking part, on the dock and on the ship (gives way to a lower deck). A hold to stand in needs about 2 m from that floor to the deck above. In `KwSole.cs`, `KwSoleDock.cs`, `KwShipColliders.cs`.

## Hammer icons look wrong
- **Problem:** rendered icons (table, rudders, hatches) were dark, oddly lit, small and showed stray parts.
- **Cause:** the icon camera rendered with the world's sun, fog, ambient and shader light values left from loading; it drew hidden children; it framed by box corners from the bow.
- **Solution:** its own forward-rendered layer with the world's lights and fog off for the shot and restored after, only what a placed piece shows, framed to the real vertices from the side; the fixed pieces wear painted icons. In `KwIcons.cs`, `KwIcon*.cs`, `KwPaintedIcons.cs`.

## Blueprint table finds no dock by keel blocks
- **Problem:** the table could not be placed by a ship built on loose keel blocks ("none are near").
- **Cause:** only dock sections and keel block guides counted as a dry dock.
- **Solution:** a row of keel blocks (same heading, in line, at most 5 m apart) is a dock too, measured from its aft block to its fore one. In `KwBpDock.cs`.

## Dock never empty on keel block guides
- **Problem:** "build on the dock" never worked on a guide dock, and guides were saved with the ship.
- **Cause:** keel block guides were counted as ship pieces.
- **Solution:** guides count as dock pieces. In `KwBpDock.cs`.

## Deck pattern repeats
- **Problem:** decks showed the same texture tile every 4 m, and a row of equal deck parts laid identical boards.
- **Cause:** one planar UV across the deck; the board layout came only from the part's shape.
- **Solution:** boards cut at staggered lengths, each from its own spot in the texture, seeded by a random number saved in the part's text (old decks read seed 0). In `KwDeckBoards.cs`, `KwDeckLayout.cs`, `KwSpan.cs`.

## Deck runs to a point past the bow and stern
- **Problem:** on a ship with a thin keel a deck reaching a stem ran on past the post to a sharp, flat point, a metre outside the hull at each end (on the dock and on the launched ship).
- **Cause:** where a deck's width runs out between two stations its end was put where the keel line, carried on, would climb through the deck. Over a thin keel (0.1 m wide: 5 cm each side of the centre line, under the planking's 8 cm thickness) the hull's inside closes at the stem's tip while the keel is still far under the deck, so that point lay well past the part's end. The saved spans were right; only the drawing was wrong.
- **Solution:** the keel line is used only when it reaches the deck between those two stations; otherwise the deck ends where its width runs out between them, found by halving the way (as the hold floor's ends are). Decks, their colliders and their cost all take the edge from there. In `KwShapes.cs`.

## Camera over the head and bobbing in the hold; sea in the hold
- **Problem:** below decks the camera sat above the player's head and rose and fell with the waves while he stood still, and water showed inside the hull.
- **Cause:** the game keeps its camera 0.3 m over the water's surface, waves included; a hold floor is below the waterline, so the camera was pushed up to the sea and rode it, and from there the sea's surface lay inside the hull in front of the water mask. On deck the game also pins the camera to the body on a ship, so every heave moved the view.
- **Solution:** under a Broadside deck or with the eye below the sea on our ship the view is the player's eyes (body as shadow only, near plane in); the water mask (below) keeps the sea out of the hull, never by hiding the sea itself (that hid it outside the ship too); on deck the camera's height lags the ship's heave by 0.6 s. In `KwShipEyes.cs`, `KwShipSteady.cs`.

## Ship tips over and stops in a side wind
- **Problem:** under sail with the wind on the beam a tall ship heeled about 25° and stopped dead.
- **Cause:** the game's sail push leans half into the wind's way and was applied whole at the hull's full depth (8.65 m on a galleon) over the centre of weight, which sat at the waterline: a big sideways lever with little to right it.
- **Solution:** only the push along the ship drives it, a fifth of the depth (0.5–2 m) over the centre of weight; a quarter of the sideways push gets past the keel, at the centre of weight; the centre of weight sits 35% of the way up to the waterline (ballast). H at the helm nudges a stuck ship free. In `KwShipDrive.cs`, `KwShipHydro.cs`, `KwShipTuning.cs`, `KwShipNudge.cs`.

## Ship looks dry down to the keel
- **Problem:** a launched ship floated at its waterline, but only the keel looked wet: the sea was not drawn against the hull's underwater side.
- **Cause (uncertain):** first taken to be the water mask filling the hull from keel to sheer. But the game's `Custom/WaterMask` is depth-tested (drawn after solids, only where nothing solid is in front, writing stencil 2), so from outside it cannot hide the sea in front of the hull. More likely the clear Meadows water showed the hull under it plainly (the swimming player's legs show as clearly in the same shot), together with the bow-up trim fixed in "Ships sit bow-up or bow-down".
- **Solution:** the mask is a thin slab inside the hull round the waterline (0.5 m under to 0.6 m over), as the game's longship's mask is; it hides the water inside the hull from above and from the hold. Check again in game in deeper, darker water before changing anything more. In `KwShipWaterMask.cs`.
- **Since:** the depth test holds, but the mask itself showed from outside: see "A sinking ship looked dry below the sea".

## Planks stretch on a tall stern
- **Problem:** on a tall stern castle (the sheer climbing from 5 m amidships to 8.6 m) the planks grew taller with the hull and their texture stretched up with them; round a curving stem it stretched along the hull too.
- **Cause:** a section's own points were its strakes: every section had its family's six to eight strakes from keel to sheer, each laid on one 16 px row of the texture however tall (1.1 m amidships, 1.8 m at the stern, against the row's 0.5 m), and u ran by the part's length along its axis, not the strake's own.
- **Solution:** strakes one texture row (0.5 m at the game's 32 px a metre) wide round the hull, cut from each station's girth measured from the keel, so a deeper section has more strakes and the upper ones run out against the sheer; the texture's rows sit on the real laps, and a rib shared by two parts cuts the same strakes. Along, u is each strake's own length from the part's first rib, counted the way the strake runs at each station. A flat stern's transom board is planked in rows of the same width. The dock part, the preview, the ghost and the launched ship all build from it. In `KwGirth.cs`, `KwStrakes.cs`, `KwClip.cs`, `KwPlanks.cs`, `KwUv.cs`.
- **Proof:** offline on the pirate ship, planking area within 15% of 32 px a metre both ways: 0% before, 85-88% after (the rest is the lips, and the bow stem's last 1.5 m, where the keel sweeps up into the post and the strakes narrow, by up to half at the post, as real hood ends do).
- **Since:** the sides' strakes are laid level instead of round from the keel ("Boards sweep up at the stems"); the rows and the transom board's rows stay as here; u along each strake is laid along the ship since "Planking repeats and jumps at the seams".

## Boards sweep up at the stems
- **Problem:** the user: "Is there a way to make the texture like horizontal boards that don't do a vertical uplift, so it looks more like real wood for the ship?" With strakes measured round from the keel, every strake swept up with the keel into a stem or a raised stern, running steeply up and narrowing in the last metre or two, like boards bending skyward.
- **Cause:** a strake measured round the section from the keel climbs wherever the keel climbs: every edge over a rising keel rises as far.
- **Solution:** strakes are cut by a measure that is height on the hull's upright sides and girth on its bottom: worked down from the sheer (its height over the level keel line), each section segment counts its rise where it stands steeper than 55° from flat, its length where flatter than 35°, and a smooth blend between, so the two meet softly at the turn of the bilge. The side strakes' edges lie at fixed heights, one every 0.5 m over the level keel line, and run level fore and aft at the same heights on every part; a board ends where the rising keel, a stem post or the sheer cuts it (hood ends); the bottom's boards stay 0.5 m round, and no board is wider than 0.62 m (where the blend meets a 51° side). The level keel line is a part's z = 0, except on a part starting partway along a stem, whose rib stands on the climbed keel: its line is as far under as the keel has climbed there (`KwSpan.Level`, `KwDef.Level`), so the dock, the preview, the ghost and the launched ship, all built from the spans, lay one ship's boards at the same heights. Strakes are numbered from the keel line (strake 0 has its foot at its height on the sides; the bottom's lowest are below 0, and come round the texture's rows too), so a painted band is the same level strakes on every part; the band window counts from 1 there, and the bottom's strakes under it are not painted. The lowest board, cut by the keel at any width, stands the lap proud along the keel as a lower edge does, so it still closes on the keel; without that, 1 and 2 of 23,184 rays got out over 2 and 4 m flat sterns' keels, 2 from the pirate ship's hold. A flat stern's transom board is planked in rows at the same heights, and a castle's raised sides are level strakes from its keel line too. In `KwGirth.cs` (`Measure`), `KwStrakes.cs`, `KwPlanks.cs`, `KwSpan.cs`, `KwMesh.cs`, `KwBandMesh.cs`, `KwBandField.cs`, `KwUv.cs`.
- **Proof:** offline, the pirate ship and the Medium rendered side on and from the bow quarter, before and after: the side boards run level along the whole ship and end cleanly against the stems and the stern's rising keel; every part's board edges at its ends lie at the keel line plus a whole number of 0.5 m (the pirate's bow part, its rib 4 cm up its keel end, included: before, its strakes stood 4 cm off the next part's); parts meeting at a rib with the same section meet to 0.1 mm (the two seams that differ are the blueprints' own sections, as before); a band "9-10" lies level at 5.4-6.4 m on every part of the pirate ship (before it climbed to 8.9 m at the bow); texture within 15% of 32 px a metre: the pirate's bow part 38% before, 81% after, the Medium 54-55% after, 80-87% before (its sides lean, so its boards are up to a quarter wider than their texture row); a castle's raised sides build; rays from inside flat sterns climbing 0.5, 2, 4 and 8 m (6 m long) and 8 m (16 m long), and from the pirate ship's hold: none of 23,184 and 356,868 get out (2 of the pirate's did before the keel kept its lap).

## Headsails had nowhere to set
- **Problem:** jibs could not be made: a jib sets on a stay from the foremast down to the bowsprit, and a mast's saved text cannot know where the bowsprit's tip is (nor the bowsprit's where the mast is); the triangle sail's cloth also has its luff and foot square to each other, and a jib's are not.
- **Cause:** every part is built from its own text alone, and the sail code's cloth, its motion, folds and bolt rope are all worked out in the cloth's own square axes.
- **Solution:** the bowsprit saves its own tip (length and rise) and its jibs; the stay's upper end is found whenever the jibs are built, from the ship's own pieces: the masts loaded round it on the dock (looked for again every 2 s, so a mast built later takes its jibs), the blueprint's masts on a launched ship and for its cost and drive. The triangle sail's cloth is set into the jib's shape by the shear taking its x to the foot and its z up the stay, split (polar, then the stretch's own axes) into a turn, a stretch and a turn back on three nested transforms, so the cloth, its wind, folds and bolt rope stay the sail code's own. In `KwJibStay.cs`, `KwJibShear.cs`, `KwJibPlan.cs`, `KwJibDress.cs`.
- **Proof:** offline, the shear through Unity's own quaternion maths lands every cloth point where the jib's plane puts it (within 2 mm, plane turned or not); 18 bowsprits against a 10 m foremast cut jibs with their clews over the deck, heads over a metre forward of the mast and the outer jib wholly forward of the inner stay.

## Scaled ship's rudder and caps off their posts
- **Problem:** building a saved ship back scaled (at the blueprint table), every piece scaled up from the keel line put the rudder and the post caps beside their posts: on the pirate ship at 50% the rudder sat 0.25 m too high.
- **Cause:** a keel's timber keeps its depth (a timber size) and its post its thickness along the keel, so the rudder's point (the post's foot, under the keel line) and the post's top (on the keel's curve a post's thickness back from its tip) do not scale with the ship about the keel line.
- **Solution:** each keel's post top and rudder point are worked out before and after (`KwKeel.Custom`'s places), and a cap, a bowsprit or a rudder moves by its own post's change; a keel's top, not its foot, is scaled from the keel line. A stem's "top the post allows the hull" keeps its clearance under the post. In `KwBpPosts.cs`, `KwBpResize.cs`, `KwBpSpanText.cs`.
- **Proof:** offline the pirate ship's dragon cap sits on its post's top before (10.4 m, measured) and after at 50% (5.69 m against 5.9 m from plain scaling), its rudder on the stern post's foot.

## Ships sit bow-up or bow-down
- **Problem:** launched ships floated tilted along their length: the pirate ship 0.83 m bow-up over its 15.6 m, a 10 m flat-stern hull 0.54 m bow-down, the Medium 0.13 m bow-up; and a ship floated differently depending on which way round it was saved (the pirate ship turned round sat 0.10 m bow-down instead).
- **Cause:** two things. The centre of weight sat on the hull's middle along its length, not over the middle of the water the hull displaces at its design waterline (the centre of buoyancy), so any hull with unlike ends (a flat stern, a full bow, a high stern castle) tilted until the two lined up. And each buoyancy slice had the shape of the section at its aft end, so a hull narrowing into its bow got too much lift there and too little at a narrowing stern, and the bias turned with the ship. The dock waterline copied the same numbers.
- **Solution:** each slice is shaped as the hull is at its middle: its two sections' starboard halves read at the same shares of their girth (every corner of both kept), averaged and mirrored (`KwSectionBlend`); the water mask still lofts the sections themselves. The weight and its centre are worked out once (`KwShipWeight`): the water displaced to the design waterline, its centre straight over that water's middle along and across the ship, still 35% of the way up to the waterline; the launched ship's body, the dock waterline and the table's figures all take it from there. In `KwShipWeight.cs`, `KwSectionBlend.cs`, `KwHullSlices.cs`, `KwShipHydro.cs`, `KwWaterlineLevel.cs`.
- **Proof:** offline, heave and pitch runs of `KwShipHydro`'s force law: the Medium, the pirate ship, an 18 m shared ship, a symmetric hull and the flat-stern hull all rest level (within 1 mm over their length) at their design waterline, both ways round; the dock's line agrees. The middle shapes alone float the symmetric hull level and the pirate ship the same both ways (0.38 and 0.37 m bow-up with the weight still on the middle). Flooding lists and sinks the ships as before (the Medium's deck under after 1398 s through a 12 cm hole, 322 s through a 25 cm one).

## Slit along a high flat stern's keel
- **Problem:** since a flat stern may climb any height, a high one's planking stood off its keel between stations: a see-through slit along both of the keel's top edges, wider the higher the stern (found looking into holes the user saw low on the pirate ship's 2 m stern).
- **Cause:** the planking's bottom edge runs straight from station to station while the keel climbs as t², so between n even stations it stands up to rise / (4 n²) off the keel. Flat sterns were always drawn in six stretches: 3.5 mm off on the first 0.5 m ones (the planking's 2.5 cm lap closes that on the keel), 1.4 cm at 2 m, 5.6 cm at 8 m.
- **Solution:** a flat stern is drawn in as many stretches as keep it within the first ones' 3.5 mm: six up to 0.5 m, twelve at 2 m, 24 at 8 m (single-ended and keel-end-to-keel-end parts, on the dock and on launched ships, which build again from their saved spans). Stems and 0.5 m sterns keep their stations. The colliders, buoyancy slices and water mask come from the same stations, so they follow the climbing bottom more closely too (the hold's floor boxes up to 16 cm off it at 2 m, not 31). In `KwShapes.cs` (`TransomSteps`).
- **Proof:** offline, rays from inside a 6 m flat stern's hull over its keel in every downward direction: 17, 76 and 274 of 15,624 got out at rises of 2, 4 and 8 m before (404 at 8 m on a 16 m keel), 1 (grazing the keel's corner), 0 and 0 after; a 0.5 m stern 0 both times. The pirate ship's saved file: 12 of 178,848 before, 1 after. Otherwise its stern was already closed: planking, transom board and keel meet with no open seam and no folded triangle, so the larger pale triangles in that screenshot were not openings in the hull.

## Patterns off the edges of reefed sails
- **Problem:** a pattern painted into a sail's texture (a border, an emblem) would have slid off a reefed gaff or triangle sail: the border along a triangle's after edge ran diagonally across the reefed sail, and a gaff's head showed texture from above the gaff.
- **Cause:** a sail along the ship lowered on its boom showed its texture cut off at a height (a gaff's rows shifted up, not following the gaff), and a triangle's standing part, drawn full width at any hoist, showed rows of texture as wide as the whole foot, so its after edge was off the cloth's diagonal in the texture by 0.5 at half sail, 0.8 at a fifth.
- **Solution:** the texture stays on the cloth: what stands shows the upper share of the cloth's texture (a gaff's rows under its gaff line), the flakes on the boom the rest, row by row; a patterned triangle's rows are taken as wide as the cloth is there (stretched across the reefed sail), while a plain one keeps its old, unstretched mapping. Square sails already worked this way. In `KwSailCanvas.cs` (`AlongUv`, `FlakeUv`), `KwSailDrape.cs`.
- **Proof:** offline, at hoists 1, 0.5 and 0.2: a patterned triangle's after edge on the diagonal and a gaff's head on its gaff line to 1e-4, the flakes meeting the standing cloth; the painted borders and emblems laid on the reefed geometry sit on its edges.

## Flooded ship dives by the head
- **Problem:** once launched ships floated level, the pirate ship flooded to about 40% went from a few degrees to 40° bow-down and stayed there (its pitch moment had rest points near 12° and 40°); a big unbailed hole would have taken it there.
- **Cause:** the hold's water had one level surface the whole length of the ship, following its pitch fully, so the slightest trim ran tonnes of water to the low end (the free surface effect along a 15 m hold). Compartments with the bulkhead tops at the waterline alone did not cure it: the water over the tops was one free surface again (38° at 40%), and the water held under them kept a holed end full (13° at 20%).
- **Solution:** the hold is split into compartments at the planking parts' ends (1.2 to 4 m apart), each with its own volume and surface, its holes letting the sea into it alone; every surface lies level across the ship (1.2 s late: the slosh and list as before) but follows only a fifth of the pitch along it; water passes to the lower neighbour slowly, seeping through the bulkhead under its top (the design waterline) and pouring over it. The ZDO keeps each compartment's volume (`kw_flood_parts`; `kw_flood` alone is spread at one level); B bails the compartment stood in; each compartment's water is drawn with its end against a bulkhead. In `KwFloodHolds.cs`, `KwBulkheads.cs`, `KwFloodLevel.cs`, `KwShipFlood.cs`, `KwShipBail.cs`, `KwFloodSurface.cs`, `KwFloodFaces.cs`.

## Nothing could be built on a launched ship
- **Problem:** the user: "I want a way to build things like cannons and the weapon mounts on the ship after it is built, and not while it is being built on the keel guide with keel blocks." The ship's blueprint is read exactly as packed (`KwBpFile.Exact` from `KwShipData.Unpack`: nothing left out, nothing turned; it was turned bow forward once, at launch); the ships folder's files still go through `KwBpFile.Read`. In `Sea/KwSea*.cs`, `Blueprints/KwBpFile.cs`, `Ships/KwShipData.cs`.
- **Proof:** offline (`ov_SEA_harness` in the session's scratchpad, 93 checks on the saved bubba, Pirate_ship and medium): an owner and a second peer through three railing edits (a side taken, a railing removed, the same built back), every hole keeping its number, part and patch, the rig's and other railings' marks kept, the shields on their hooks, the peer's ship the owner's after each, the cost and refund once, a break-up after the edits giving back exactly launch cost plus the edits; a ship with tombstones and a backward rig unpacking with every piece at its index, and the unpacking's call graph reaching neither the unknown-prefab filter nor the bow fix (Unity's objects cannot be made offline). ## Doors and windows could not be cut on a launched ship
- **Problem:** the user (2026-10-26): "add in a porthole window to put in another cannon". The Doorway and Window tools were to cut, change and fill in openings in a launched ship's planking and cabins as on the dock, the opening cut into the look and the boxes, its cover working, the flooding seeing it.
- **Cause:** the tools read loaded pieces in the world: the part a ray hit, its ZDO text, its transform for every point, rays down for the deck inside, the dock's other pieces for the planking in line and a doorway's partners. A launched ship is one body; its planking and cabins are blueprint entries, their looks packed with the decks into a few hull chunks and their frames in the ship's one combined mesh, so one part could neither be drawn again nor hidden for the ghost. Its covered openings are numbered in blueprint order (`kw_ship_open<n>`), so cutting one renumbers every opening after it: a peer reading the new states before it drew the new ship would swing the wrong leaves, and a door opened by a peer still on the old numbers would open another. And the flooding found the ship's openings component once, at build; a ship launched with no openings had none.
- **Solution:** the tools' own code runs at sea with a part standing for the piece: each planking part and cabin read from the blueprint, its walls seated on the ship's keels, every site's `Of` the part (`KwSeaOpeningPart`), so a plan tells parts apart as it does pieces; what the dock asks of the world comes from a context of plain maths in the ship's frame (`KwSeaOpeningShip`, `KwSeaOpeningFloor`, `KwSeaOpeningMaps` through the dock's own map, `KwOpeningHull.Mapped`); the aim is the core's ray in the ship's frame (`KwSeaOpeningAim`). Planking parts and cabins are drawn each on its own, one renderer a material, and handed to the hole cutter by index (`KwSeaOpeningLooks`, `KwHoleLooks.Own`), their boxes kept by index (`KwSeaOpeningBoxes`, `KwHoleBoxes.Rebox`), so an edit replaces one part and the ghost hides one part. The owner moves every covered opening's state to its new number in the frame it writes the blueprint (`KwShipOpeningPlan.States`); every peer reads the states only while the ship's revision is the one its leaves were numbered in, and a door's call carries that revision; the leaves of unchanged parts are renumbered, not made again (`KwShipOpenings.Replan`). The openings component is on every ship. In `Sea/KwSeaOpening*.cs`, `Ships/KwShipOpenings.cs`, `KwShipOpeningPlan.cs`, `KwShipOpeningLeaf.cs`, `KwHoleLooks.cs`, `KwHoleBoxes.cs`, `KwShipColliders.cs`, `KwShipHouses.cs`, and the tools' `Tools/KwOpening*.cs`, `KwDoorwayTool.cs`, `KwWindowTool.cs`.
- **Proof:** offline (`ov_OPN_harness` in the session's scratchpad, 277 checks on the saved bubba, Pirate_ship and medium): aimed from outside, a gunport, a Shift row of portholes, a door in a cabin's wall or the planking and a doorway through a cabin's raised side placed, edited, Shift-edited and filled in through the sea edit's package, the owner's check and a second peer; rays through every new opening pass the part's boxes and through every filled-in one are stopped; every state kept with its opening; holes in an opening's place dropped with their patches, the others kept; the cost the openings' own. Not yet seen in game.

## Climbing down from a crow's nest kills
- **Problem:** Shift+E from the crow's nest down to the deck killed the player.
- **Cause:** the climb moves the player at once (10–16 m down); the game still held the nest's height as the fall's start (`Character.m_maxAirAltitude`) and charged a fall from there on the next landing.
- **Solution:** after every Broadside teleport-climb the fall's start is set to where the player lands (`KwMastClimb`, `KwLadderClimb`, `KwShipLadderLanding`).

## Boarding ladder drops the player back into the sea
- **Problem:** climbing a launched ship's boarding ladder put the player aboard ("Player onboard") and a second later back in the water ("over board").
- **Cause:** the climb's spot is planned from the blueprint for a 0.22 m body, but the game's player is 0.49 m round; on a full-bodied hull the hull's collider boxes stand inside the drawn planking, and pieces built aboard (cannons) are not in the blueprint. The player landed overlapping a hull box and the physics pushed him out over the side.
- **Solution:** after the game's ladder places the player on a Broadside ship, the player's own capsule is tried there against the ship's solid colliders and moved inboard and along in small steps to the nearest clear spot on the ship's floor (`KwShipLadderLanding`, a postfix on `Ladder.Interact`).

## Climbing onto a roof drags the player back down
- **Problem:** the user (2026-10-26): climbing a cabin's ladder put the player on the roof, then a moment later back down on the deck ("it tries to place me, fails and then just puts me back down"); a roof climb on a cabin on a cabin did the same.
- **Cause:** the game keeps a player standing still on a ship at the spot they stood on it (`Character.ApplyGroundForce`: pulled back at ten times the distance a second from up to 4 m away, or set straight onto it when another peer owns the ship), and takes that spot again only when the ship body under them changes. Deck and roof are one body, so after the climb the spot was still the ladder's foot, 3 m away: the player was dragged back toward it over the roof's edge. Recorded in game: no input, the push pointed at the ladder's foot, the game's spot sat there.
- **Solution:** every Broadside climb puts the player down through `KwClimbRoom.Place`, which also lets go of that spot (`m_lastAttachBody`), so the game takes it again where they land (`KwLadderClimb`, `KwMastClimb`, `KwShipLadderLanding`).

## Can't walk through a roof railing's ladder gap
- **Problem:** the gap a roof railing leaves where a ladder comes up could not be walked through, and a climb onto the roof there could push the player back off.
- **Cause:** the gap was 0.7 m (`KwRoofRail.Gap`) less its posts, narrower than the player (0.98 m).
- **Solution:** gaps are 1.2 m. Gaps are worked out from the ladder's place on read, so every saved roof railing gets the wider gap.

## Boarding ladder half on, half off a curved side
- **Problem:** on a stem or stern the boarding ladder hung with one rail on the planking and the other in the air.
- **Cause:** the ladder always hung square to the ship (straight out sideways) at the hull's widest across its width; where the side curves in toward a stem, one end of it stands off.
- **Solution:** places where the side runs in or out by more than 6 cm a metre rank after every straight one (the runs between ribs, the user's preference); a ladder that must hang on a curve is turned square to the side there (`KwShipLadderPlan.Slope`), so both rails lie on it. Ranks are worked out once per place (`KwShipLadderFind.Ranked`).

## Can't walk through cabin doors
- **Problem:** open or shut, a cabin's door could not be walked through.
- **Cause:** the single door was 1 m wide (0.8 m the least), its frame leaving about 0.9 m, and the game's player is 0.98 m across (capsule radius 0.49); the ship generator also hung boarding ladders right beside doors.
- **Solution:** a single door is 1.3 m wide, a double 2.2 m, a door at least 1.2 m and an open doorway at least 1.2 m (`KwOpeningShape`, `KwOpeningTypes`); the broadside-ships generator keeps ladders 0.5 m clear of doors. Doors cut before keep their width: cut them again wider.

## Dropped items fall through the ship
- **Problem:** an item dropped on a launched ship's deck fell through it to the sea floor.
- **Cause:** the ship's boxes are on the vehicle layer, as the game's own ships' are, and the game's physics never lets the item layer meet the vehicle layer.
- **Solution:** a copy of each box under `ship/item_floors` (`KwShipItemFloors`, made with the boxes) on the game's UI layer, which meets items and terrain but not the water, characters, the camera, the building rays or the shot trace. A first try put the copies on the item layer itself, which also meets the water surface: every ship sat on top of the sea.

## A ship under sail stops dead
- **Problem:** sailing at 16 km/h with the wind behind, over deep water, the ship stopped dead.
- **Cause:** the launched ship is held still, its speed zeroed and its gravity off, until the game says the area round it is loaded (`ZNetScene.IsAreaReady`), and that was asked every physics step. The game's check fails for a moment whenever an object in the 3 by 3 sectors round the ship is not made yet, as it is each time the ship sails into new sectors. A second way: a ship with nobody counted aboard is braked to a stop, and the onboard trigger reached only 2 m over the sheer, so a player in a crow's nest was not counted.
- **Solution:** a ship released once is never held again while it stays loaded (`KwShipHydro.Hold`); the onboard trigger reaches 25 m over the sheer (`KwShipFittings.Aloft`), as the break-up's aboard check does.

===== SUMMARY (plain text, one paragraph) =====
Creatures spawn with mutations that change how the fight goes: Mad ones are twice as fast on half the health, Bloated ones explode when killed, Splintering ones split in two, and a Devouring one eats the pack around it until it comes for you. Stars beyond vanilla's two, a world tier that climbs with every boss you defeat, and tamed creatures that pass their traits on to their young. Tunable per biome, server synced and hot reloaded.

===== DESCRIPTION (BBCode) =====
[size=4][b]Description[/b][/size]
Elite Creatures Reborn makes the creature in front of you worth reading before you swing at it. Creatures spawn with named, visible mutations that change how a fight goes rather than only how long it takes, and with stars beyond the two the plain game gives out. A Mad greydwarf comes at you twice as fast on half the health. A Bloated draugr takes twice the killing and then explodes in your face. A Devouring wolf eats the boars around it and grows on what it eats until it comes looking for you.

This version is a rebuild from scratch and is not an update to earlier releases: none of the old code remains. It covers mutations, stars, world tiers, breeding, loot and boss aspects. The rest of the mod's features return over the next releases.

[size=4][b]Mutations[/b][/size]
Thirteen, one per creature by default, each with its own colour and its own word before the creature's name.
[list]
[*][b]Mad[/b] - far faster, half health.
[*][b]Bloated[/b] - double health, explodes two seconds after it dies. Smoke rides the corpse until it blows, and the corpse goes with the blast, dropping its loot there.
[*][b]Cloaked[/b] - invisible beyond 10 metres (15 for trolls and lox). Drakes are never Cloaked.
[*][b]Splintering[/b] - splits into two weaker copies when killed, which can split again. A tamed one splits into two tamed copies that keep its name and follow the same player.
[*][b]Leeching[/b] - regenerates, and heals from the damage it deals.
[*][b]Warding[/b] - reflects part of each hit back and knocks you back. It reflects the health the hit actually took, without sneak-attack or stagger bonuses, and never more than 7.5% of your maximum health.
[*][b]Plated[/b] - armoured while healthy, hits harder as that armour goes.
[*][b]Miasmic[/b] - trails poison clouds; poisons players, never creatures.
[*][b]Devouring[/b] - kills creatures in one bite and keeps their health and damage, until it is big enough to hunt you.
[*][b]Thieving[/b] - steals an item with each landed hit, up to one per star (at least one, eight at most), never equipped gear. Every stolen item shows on its nameplate; kill it and everything drops back out.
[*][b]Gilded[/b] - the loot goblin, and the rarest. It glitters gold from well beyond nameplate range, never attacks a player, and runs from any player it can see within 30 metres, so sneak up on it. Catch it and it drops three times its loot plus 20 coins for each star plus one, in every loot mode. A tamed one stays calm and drops ordinary loot.
[*][b]Blinking[/b] - every 30 seconds of a fight it vanishes and reappears 4 metres behind its target, facing it. Half a second before, the spot flashes and chimes, so you can turn in time. It never lands in a wall, off a ledge or anywhere it could not have walked to. 25% less health.
[*][b]Relentless[/b] - once it picks you it keeps coming while you are within 150 metres: it heads for where you are even out of sight, sneaking does not hide you, it swims after you, and it does not despawn at dawn while it hunts. It is never faster than its base speed, so outrun it past 150 metres or it follows you home.
[/list]
Every number is a field in the rule file, and any mutation can be switched off there.

[size=4][b]Stars[/b][/size]
Beyond vanilla's two, counted in fives on the nameplate: a small star is one, a large star is five. A mutation on a large star is stronger. Star chances and mutation chances are set per biome, so the Meadows stay the Meadows. Bosses have their own star table, and a boss's stars are drawn under its health bar.

[size=4][b]World tiers[/b][/size]
The world starts at tier 0 and goes up one tier the first time each boss is defeated - by anyone, in any order - so the seven bosses take it to tier 7. Killing a boss again changes nothing, and every rise is announced to everyone on the server. Each tier makes stars and mutations more common in every biome while keeping each biome's character: by tier 7 the share of unstarred Meadows creatures falls from 73 in 100 to 44, five-star Plains creatures rise from 5 in 100 to 22, and every mutation chance doubles. Bosses are unaffected, and creatures already alive keep what they rolled. A world that killed bosses before the mod was installed starts at that tier. The inventory shows the tier on a globe plate under the weight, and every plate on that side names itself when hovered. The off switch, which bosses count (modded ones too) and the boost per tier are in the rule file.

[size=4][b]Breeding[/b][/size]
Tamed creatures keep their traits and pass them on. A newborn - a pup, a piglet, a calf, or an egg and the chick that hatches from it - takes one of its parents' mutations whenever either has one, and a star count from 0 up to its stronger parent's, every count equally likely. Two plain parents have plain young, so a strong line stays strong only if you keep the good ones. Young creatures keep their traits when they grow up, and eggs keep theirs when carried and say what they will hatch. A bred mutation behaves exactly as it does in the wild - a Miasmic pet still trails poison.

[size=4][b]Boss aspects[/b][/size]
A boss never takes a mutation. It takes an [b]aspect[/b], named before the boss ("Enraged Eikthyr") and shown at its altar before you summon: hover the offering bowl for the current aspect, what it does, what it pays, and how long until it shifts. Every altar shifts to a different aspect each in-game hour, so a group can wait for the fight it wants. The Queen and console-spawned bosses roll theirs when they appear.
[list]
[*][b]Reflective[/b] - 15% of each hit you land comes back to you as true damage.
[*][b]Shielded[/b] - 30% less damage from arrows and bolts.
[*][b]Mending[/b] - regenerates 0.3% of its health every second, in combat too.
[*][b]Summoner[/b] - calls two 2-star creatures of its own biome each time it loses 33% of its health.
[*][b]Elementalist[/b] - 20% more elemental damage.
[*][b]Enraged[/b] - 20% more physical damage.
[*][b]Twin[/b] - comes as two sharing one health pool, 25% less health and damage each; both drop full loot.
[*][b]Phantom[/b] - at 66% and 33% health splits off one copy per player online, with 25 health per world tier and half its damage; they drop nothing and vanish when the boss dies, and their small health bars sit under the boss's.
[*][b]Adaptive[/b] - takes 50% less of whichever damage type hit it most in the last 15 seconds, and glows that type's colour. Swap weapons, or spread the group across damage types.
[*][b]Fixated[/b] - marks one player, with a red eye over their head and a line in chat, and hits them 50% harder and everyone else 30% softer. Every 30 seconds the mark moves to whoever hurt it most; alone, you are always marked.
[*][b]Stormbound[/b] - every 20 seconds a glowing circle appears under each player within 40 metres, and 2 seconds later lightning strikes it: 8% of your maximum health and a stagger if you are still inside.
[*][b]Gravitic[/b] - every 20 seconds it roars and drags every player within 30 metres toward it for 1.5 seconds, then slams: 10% of your maximum health and a stagger within 6 metres of it. Melee players get a free gap-closer; archers lose their distance.
[*][b]Colossal[/b] - 40% bigger, 15% more health, 15% slower. Its heavy blows send out a shockwave that knocks players within 8 metres down; roll through it or jump it.
[/list]
About one boss fight in five stays plain. Harder aspects pay more: each has its own loot multiplier, from x1.1 for Shielded to x1.5 for Summoner. Stormbound's lightning, Gravitic's slam and Colossal's shockwave are dodged with a roll, never blocked with a shield. Every number, the chances, the shift interval and each boss's summons are in the rule file.

[size=4][b]Boss damage board[/b][/size]
When a boss dies, everyone on the server sees who fought it: the boss's name and every player who hurt it, with the health each one took off it, most first, at the far left of the screen for a minute. It counts what the boss actually lost - after its resistances, without the overkill - and only players count. Twin bosses are one fight and one board, and on a Phantom boss's board the damage dealt to its copies counts too. Each player can turn it off or change how long it stays. Type [b]/damage[/b] in chat to see the latest board again, even with the board turned off; a player who joined after the kill gets it from the server.

[size=4][b]Installation[/b][/size]
[list=1]
[*]Install BepInExPack for Valheim.
[*]Install Elite Creatures Reborn on the server and on every client.
[*]Start the game once. The config file gglasgow.elitecreaturesreborn.cfg and the rule file creature_rules.yml are written to BepInEx/config, both documented.
[*]Edit either while playing. Changes apply a few seconds after saving.
[/list]
For manual install, drop EliteCreaturesReborn.dll into BepInEx/plugins. The mod is standalone: it does not read the config files, save data or API of any other mod. If you used a different creature star mod before, remove it and its config files first.

[size=4][b]Configuration[/b][/size]
The config file holds each player's display preferences: star colours and sizes, whether trait names show, nameplate distance, effect density, stolen-item icons, the boss damage board and the world tier plate. The rule file holds the rules: star chances and star power, mutation chances and strength per biome and per creature, a switch for each mutation, boss stars and aspects, world tiers, breeding, loot and respawning. Both are written and documented on first run and both hot reload while you play.

A creature can have mutation rules of its own: an entry under [b]creatures:[/b] in the rule file, matched by prefab name, takes [b]mutation chance[/b], [b]mutation chances[/b] and [b]mutation power[/b] just as a biome does, for that creature only, in whatever biome it is in. Everything the entry does not name still comes from the biome, and bosses ignore these entries. This ends Cloaked mosquitoes without changing the rest of the Plains:
[code]creatures:
  - match: Deathsquito
    mutation chances:
      Cloaked: [0][/code]
A new rule file ships with three such entries: trolls and lox show themselves at 15 metres instead of 10, and drakes (Hatchling) are never Cloaked.

The server's rule file binds every connected player; display preferences stay with each player and are never locked.

[size=4][b]Console commands[/b][/size]
Run from the in-game console (F5).
[code]elite spawn <prefab> <stars> [mutation...]  a creature in front of you with chosen traits, bypassing every roll
                                            (a boss takes one aspect instead: elite spawn Bonemass 2 Twin)
elite inspect                               the resolved stars, mutations or aspect, and numbers for the creature under your crosshair
elite purge                                 remove the loaded creatures this mod has marked, with no drops
                                            (a Thieving creature's stolen goods drop first)
elite effects <text>                        list loaded effect prefabs matching the text and play one
elite reference                             write creature_reference.yml: every creature the game knows, with its drop table
elite tier                                  the world tier, what it does to the rolls, and which bosses count (any player)
damage                                      the latest boss damage board again; /damage in chat (any player)[/code]
The other elite commands are admin only.

[size=4][b]Multiplayer[/b][/size]
Creature state travels in the world data the game already shares, so every player sees the same creature with the same traits. Install the mod on the dedicated server as well as on every client. In the console, [b]charter status[/b] shows whether the server binds your settings, [b]charter diff[/b] lists where the server's values differ from your own file, and [b]charter versions[/b] lists the mods on both sides.
